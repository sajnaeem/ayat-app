Why Munib

Doomscrolling changed how we pay attention. We can use that.

Years of short videos have trained us to take things in quickly, a few seconds at a time. Sitting down with a whole page of the Quran can feel impossible, and then the guilt makes it harder still.

Munib doesn't fight that habit. It borrows it. The feed you scroll is made of ayat, each one short enough to take in, recited aloud as it appears, with the word being recited lighting up so you can follow along.

As you keep coming back, Munib gently stretches your attention: single ayat, then short passages, then whole surahs.

Why I built it

This year, after my father passed away, I set out to become a better Muslim. One of the first things I wanted was to read the Quran properly, and I struggled. I'd sit down with good intentions and lose focus within minutes.

Then I noticed how easily I could lose an hour scrolling. My attention wasn't gone. It had just been trained for something else.

Munib is the app I needed: the same scroll, pointed back towards Allah. I hope it helps you too.

Questions

Good to know

Is Munib free?

Reading the whole Quran in Munib is free forever, with no account. Munib Plus adds For you, word meanings, tafsir, memorise mode and more. It starts with 30 days free, then costs £0.99 a month or £9 a year.

I can't read Arabic. Is Munib for me?

Yes. Every ayah has a translation and a pronunciation guide you can switch on, the recitation highlights each word as it's said, and you can hold any word to see its meaning.

Is Munib for a particular group or sect?

No. Munib is for every Muslim, and for anyone curious about the Quran. It shows the Quran itself, with widely used translations and the classical Tafsir Ibn Kathir, and its own short notes avoid rulings and sectarian debates.

Which script and translations does Munib use?

The Arabic is in the Uthmani script, the style of the Madinah mushaf. Translations are Saheeh International (English), Abul A'la Maududi (Urdu), Taisirul Quran (Bengali), the Indonesian Ministry of Religious Affairs, Diyanet (Turkish), Muhammad Hamidullah (French) and Isa García (Spanish).

Where does the Quran text come from?

From the Quran Foundation (Quran.com), including the translations, the recitations and Tafsir Ibn Kathir. The short context notes on the For you feed are written for Munib and are being checked by a scholar before launch.

Does Munib work offline?

Yes, for reading. Every surah you open is saved on your phone for a week, so the text works on the Tube, on a plane or with no signal. Recitation needs an internet connection.

Is Munib suitable for children?

Reading the Quran tab is suitable for everyone. To create an account and post reflections you need to be 13 or over, and reflections follow community guidelines with reporting and blocking.

Isn't this just more screen time?

Munib is designed for coming back, not for staying. After 20 minutes it suggests a break, it never sends more than one reminder a day, and it doesn't use streaks or guilt.

When can I get it?

Munib is coming to the App Store for iPhone first, with Android after.

Can I delete my account?

Yes, at any time from the You screen in the app. It permanently deletes your account, progress, saved ayat and reflections.
